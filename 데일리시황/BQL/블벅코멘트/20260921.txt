Review Window: Monday 14 September – Friday 19 September 2026 (T0 = 19 Sep 2026) | Report cut-off: Monday 21 September 2026, 19:56 JST
Note: 1-Day return data reflects the most recently completed session (19 Sep). Bloomberg data pulled at report cut-off; US markets were open at time of query and intraday 21 Sep moves are excluded from all primary analysis.


1. GLOBAL MARKET WRAP
US equities posted a solid five-session gain, with the S&P 500 rising 1.31% and the Nasdaq (QQQ) advancing 2.37% over the Review Window, while the Russell 2000 was essentially flat (+0.06%), underscoring a large-cap, growth-led advance rather than a broad risk rally. The dominant driver was a sharp reversal in oil prices — WTI fell 5.09% over the week to $97.22, retreating from the prior week's multi-year highs above $100 — which eased stagflation fears and allowed long-duration assets to recover. The 10-year Treasury yield fell 7.2 basis points to 4.953% and the 30-year dropped 7.4 bps to 5.290%, providing relief to rate-sensitive growth stocks. The 2-year yield declined only 2.0 bps to 4.720%, leaving the curve modestly steeper. The VIX collapsed 16% to 14.87, signalling a decisive shift toward risk appetite. The dollar (DXY) was flat at 100.29. The FOMC delivered a widely anticipated 25 basis-point rate hike on Wednesday 16 September — its first since 2023 — but a hawkish-but-not-aggressive tone and the oil pullback combined to lift equities. Technology was the clear sector leader; Communication Services was the notable laggard.

2. THIS WEEK'S ZEITGEIST
Four narratives dominated the Review Window and carry directly into the next five sessions. First, the Fed's 25 basis-point hike on 16 September re-established policy credibility, but Goldman Sachs revised its forecast to include a second 25 bp hike in October, and the dot plot's median 2026 terminal rate of 4.125% leaves the market debating whether the hiking cycle is truly "two-and-done" or extends further — a question that Fed speakers this week, led by New York Fed President John Williams on Tuesday, will be central to answering.

6. INTER-SECTOR INSIGHTS

Growth vs. Cyclicals: IT Re-Emerges, Cyclicals Stall
Information Technology's +3.08% five-session return versus Industrials' +0.62% and Materials' -0.73% marks a clear near-term reversal of the three-month cyclical leadership. The AI capex narrative — anchored by Dell's $25 billion guidance raise and the Accenture–Anthropic deal — is providing a company-specific earnings catalyst that cyclicals lack. However, the three-month data confirms this is a rotation in progress, not a completed shift.
(21)
(22)


Technology: Mega-Cap Concentrated, AI-Driven
Technology's outperformance is not broad-based across the sector. The Magnificent 7 index is up approximately 7.4% year-to-date, but the equal-weighted gauge gained 25% in 2025, suggesting the current advance is increasingly concentrated. Chip stocks (Nvidia, MediaTek ecosystem) and AI infrastructure names (Dell, Ciena) are leading; legacy hardware and software names are less prominent. The Morgan Stanley team notes that S&P 500 forward P/E has compressed to the lowest since March at 19x, even as EPS growth for the median stock runs in the mid-teens.
(23)


Defensives: Yield Competition Dominates
Utilities (-0.53% five-day, -8.09% three-month) and Consumer Staples (-0.64% five-day, -3.71% one-month) are underperforming despite the risk-on environment, reflecting sustained yield competition with the 10-year at 4.953% and the 30-year at 5.290%. Health Care (+0.37% five-day, +12.22% three-month) is the exception, supported by earnings resilience and a defensive bid that is not purely yield-driven. The divergence between Health Care and the other defensives is the most important cross-sector signal in the defensive space.

Rate-Sensitive Divergence: Financials Resilient, REIT/Utilities Pressured
Financials (-0.13% five-day) are holding up far better than Utilities (-0.53%) and Real Estate (-0.65%) despite the shared rate-sensitivity label. The curve steepened modestly over the week (10Y fell 7.2 bps vs 2Y -2.0 bps), which is marginally supportive of bank net interest margins. Goldman's October hike call introduces uncertainty for Financials, but the sector's three-month outperformance (+4.02%) reflects earnings resilience. REITs and Utilities face the most direct headwind from the 10-year approaching 5%.

Consumer Divergence: Discretionary Recovers, Staples Lag
Consumer Discretionary (+0.77% five-day) outperformed Consumer Staples (-0.64%) over the Review Window, consistent with the August retail sales beat (+1.2% vs +0.8% est) and Deloitte's constructive holiday outlook. However, Discretionary's one-month (-5.92%) and three-month (-3.40%) returns confirm this is a bounce within a broader underperformance trend. The consumer is spending, but the sector has been weighed down by rate sensitivity and valuation compression.
