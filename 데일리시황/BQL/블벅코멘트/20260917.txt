1. GLOBAL MARKET WRAP
The S&P 500 fell 0.5% to 7,551.81 on Wednesday, its third consecutive losing session and the lowest close since July, as the Federal Reserve delivered its first rate hike in more than three years. The move was broad-based and macro-driven: most S&P 500 sectors declined, with Energy and Financials leading losses. The Bloomberg Dollar Spot Index rose 0.5%, its largest single-day gain since June. WTI crude fell more than 3% intraday — its steepest drop since early August — as Saudi Arabia moved to restore its East-West pipeline, easing some of the supply-disruption premium. VIX futures edged higher post-close. Asian equity futures pointed lower overnight, tracking Wall Street's decline. US equity futures were little changed as of the 6:05 p.m. NY close.

2. TODAY'S ZEITGEIST
Four narratives dominate the setup for Thursday's session. First, the Fed's hawkish pivot is the dominant macro force. Chair Kevin Warsh raised the federal funds rate 25 basis points to 3.75%–4.00% and the dot plot signals one additional hike in 2026, with the median year-end projection at 4.125%. Warsh's press conference struck a decidedly hawkish tone — "inflation is too high and has been for too long" — and bond markets responded by pricing in three additional hikes by mid-2027, one more than before the decision. The 10-year yield above 5% is a valuation headwind for equities, particularly rate-sensitive sectors. Second, the oil supply picture is in flux. WTI fell more than 3% on Wednesday as Saudi Arabia moved to restore partial capacity on its East-West pipeline within days, but the broader Hormuz crisis — now more than six months old — remains unresolved, with Houthi control of Bab al-Mandeb and ongoing tanker cyberattack investigations adding uncertainty. Third, the US-China summit on September 24 in Washington is approaching, with both sides reportedly discussing tariff cuts on energy and agricultural goods ahead of the Trump-Xi meeting. Goldman Sachs surveys suggest most investors view the summit as "largely symbolic," but any concrete tariff reduction announcement could be a positive catalyst for risk assets. Fourth, the US-Canada trade escalation continues, with Trump directing agencies to remove Canadian-origin items from federal procurement, and Congress passing a bill enabling tariffs on Russian oil buyers — broadening the tariff policy toolkit.

15. PRESENTATION SUMMARY

Core Message
The S&P 500 fell 0.5% on Wednesday to its lowest since July as the Federal Reserve delivered its first rate hike in three years and Chair Warsh struck a hawkish tone, pushing the 10-year Treasury yield above 5% for the first time since 2007. 4. CROSS-MARKET INSIGHTS
1. Same oil shock, opposite transmission: Japan vs. Europe
The Saudi pipeline shutdown affected both Japan and Europe, but through different channels. In Japan, the disruption boosted upstream energy names (ENEOS +5%, Idemitsu +4.3% on 16 September) as refiners reportedly accelerated Mideast crude purchases — a direct revenue benefit. In Europe, the same disruption weighed on BP (-2.6%) and Shell (-2.3%) as Brent retreated from overbought levels, while European refiners face longer voyage times and higher costs to replace Saudi crude. Japan's energy sector was a net beneficiary; Europe's was a net drag.

(106)

Chinese chip names (SMIC +4.8%, Hua Hong +5.9% on 16 September) rallied on domestic AI policy support, while Japan's global AI proxies (SoftBank -4.6%, Advantest -4.1%, Lasertec -3.8%) sold off on OpenAI valuation risk and pre-Fed caution. The same AI theme produced opposite outcomes depending on whether the exposure was to domestic Chinese policy or to global AI capex cycles.
